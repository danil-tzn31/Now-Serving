# Replacing a lost or damaged passport (adults)

## Summary
A lost passport is replaced through a renewal appointment, with a police
report, an affidavit of loss and a ₱350 penalty. If you don't have a
photocopy of the lost passport, you also need your PSA birth certificate and
are treated as a new applicant.

## Who this is for
Adults whose passport, valid or expired, was lost or damaged.

## What you need
**Lost passport:**
- Everything on the renewal list in `passport-renewal-adult`, except the
  passport itself
- A police report in English, original and photocopy
- An affidavit of loss in English, original and photocopy (notarized if the
  passport had already expired)
- A photocopy of the lost passport, if you have one
- No photocopy? Add your PSA birth certificate. You'll be processed as a new
  applicant (see `passport-new-adult`).

**Damaged passport:**
- Everything on the renewal list, including the damaged passport
- A notarized affidavit explaining what happened

## Steps
1. File a police report (lost passport only).
2. Prepare your affidavit of loss or affidavit of explanation.
3. Book an appointment at https://www.passport.gov.ph/.
4. Go to your appointment with all documents and pay the fees.

## Fees
As of 2026-10-26:
- Passport fee: ₱950 regular, ₱1,200 expedited
- Penalty for a lost or damaged passport: ₱350

## Where to apply
At the DFA office you pick when booking.

## How long it takes
The DFA page doesn't give a separate time for lost or damaged passports.
Normal processing is 12 business days (regular) or 6 to 7 business days
(expedited).

## Common problems
- **You've lost a passport before:** the same documents and penalty apply.
- **No photocopy of the old passport:** you'll need your PSA birth
  certificate and will be treated as a new applicant. The DFA may ask for more
  documents to confirm your identity.
- **Police report or affidavit not in English:** both must be in English.

## Not covered here
- Passports lost abroad: contact the nearest Philippine embassy or consulate.
- Lost passports of children: see `passport-minors`.
