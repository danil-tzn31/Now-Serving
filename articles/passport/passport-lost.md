# Replacing a lost or damaged passport (adults)

## Summary
A lost or damaged passport is replaced through a renewal appointment, with
extra documents and a ₱350 penalty fee. If you have no photocopy of the lost
passport, you'll also need your PSA birth certificate and are treated as a
new applicant.

## Who this is for
Adults whose ePassport, valid or expired, was lost, stolen, or damaged.

## What you need
**If it was lost:**
- Everything on the renewal list in `passport-renewal-adult`, except the
  passport itself
- A police report, in English
- A notarized affidavit of loss, in English
- A photocopy of the lost passport's data page, if you have one
- If you don't have a photocopy: your PSA birth certificate (you'll be
  processed as a new applicant, see `passport-new-adult`)

**If it was damaged:**
- Everything on the renewal list, including the damaged passport
- A notarized affidavit explaining how it was damaged

## Steps
1. Get the police report (lost passport only).
2. Have your affidavit of loss or affidavit of explanation notarized.
3. Book an appointment on the DFA's online passport appointment site.
4. Go to your appointment with all documents and pay the fees.

## Fees
As of 2026-10-26:
- Passport fee: ₱950 regular, ₱1,200 expedited
- Penalty for a lost or damaged passport: ₱350

## Where to apply
At the DFA office you choose when booking.

## How long it takes
Not stated on the official page for lost or damaged passports. Normal
processing is 12 business days (regular) or 6 to 7 business days
(expedited).

## Common problems
- **Lost it more than once:** the same documents apply, per the DFA page.
- **No photocopy of the old passport:** you'll need your PSA birth
  certificate and will be treated as a new applicant.
- **Affidavit or police report not in English:** the DFA asks for both in
  English.

## Not covered here
- Passports lost abroad: contact the nearest Philippine embassy or consulate.
- Lost passports of children: see `passport-minors`.
